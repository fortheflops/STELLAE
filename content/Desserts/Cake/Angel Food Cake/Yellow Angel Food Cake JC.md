## Author
Aunt Sadie
___
## Recipe
| Measurements      | Ingredients                   |
| :----------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| 6                 | [[Eggs]], separated               |
| 1 1/2 cups        | [[All-Purpose Flour]], sifted 3 times |
| 1 1/2 cups        | [[Sugar]]                       |
| 1 teaspoon        | [[Baking Powder]]               |
| 1/2 teaspoon      | [[Cream of Tartar]]             |
| 1 pinch           | [[Salt]]                        |
| 3 tablespoons     | Cold [[Water]]                  |
| 1/2 cup           | Boiled [[Water]]                |
| To Taste          | [[Flavoring]] (e.g., 1 tsp vanilla) |
___
## Instructions
1.  Prepare your ingredients. Sift the flour and baking powder together and set aside. Separate the eggs.
2.  In a large bowl, beat the egg yolks with the 3 tablespoons of cold water.
3.  Gradually add the 1 1/2 cups of sugar to the yolks and continue beating for 10 minutes or more, until the mixture is very thick.
4.  Gently mix in the 1/2 cup of boiled water, then add the flour mixture, mixing only until combined.
5.  In a separate, clean bowl, beat the egg whites with a pinch of salt until foamy.
6.  Add the flavoring and the cream of tartar, and continue beating until stiff peaks form.
7.  Gently fold the beaten egg whites into the yolk batter, one-third at a time, until just incorporated.
8.  Pour the batter into a large, ungreased tube pan.
9.  Place in the oven and bake using the following temperature sequence:
    * Bake at **300°F for 15 minutes**.
    * Increase temperature to **350°F and bake for 30 minutes**.
    * Reduce temperature back to **300°F and bake for another 15 minutes**.
10. The total bake time is approximately 1 hour and 15 minutes. Test for doneness with a skewer after the first hour.
11. Once baked, immediately invert the pan and allow the cake to cool completely upside down before removing.
___

>[!Note]
>The recipe emphasizes that the cream of tartar is "essential" and to **"Do not grease pan."** The specific, multi-temperature baking process is a key part of this vintage recipe.
