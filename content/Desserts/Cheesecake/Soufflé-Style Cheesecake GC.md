*A light and airy soufflé-style cheesecake. This vintage recipe gets its incredible lift and delicate texture from folding stiffly beaten egg whites into a rich cream cheese and vanilla base.*

## Recipe
| Measurements               | Ingredients                                        |
| :------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 ½ lbs (3 blocks)         | [[Cream Cheese]], softened                         |
| 2 cups                     | [[Sugar]]                                          |
| 3                          | [[Eggs]], separated                                |
| 1/4 cup                    | [[Milk]]                                           |
| 1/2 teaspoon               | [[Vanilla Extract]]                                |
| Optional                   | [[Crystallized Melon]] or [[Ginger]]               |
| For the base               | Your favorite crust                              |
___
## Instructions
1.  **Preparation:** Preheat your oven to 350°F. Prepare your favorite crust in a 9-inch springform pan. Ensure all ingredients are at room temperature.
2.  In a blender or a large bowl with an electric mixer, combine the softened cream cheese, sugar, vanilla, milk, and **egg yolks**. Blend for several minutes until the mixture is completely smooth.
3.  In a separate, clean bowl, beat the 3 **egg whites** with a mixer on high speed until they form stiff, glossy peaks.
4.  Gently fold the beaten egg whites into the cream cheese mixture in three additions until just combined and no white streaks remain.
5.  Pour the filling into the prepared crust.
6.  Bake for **45-50 minutes**. The cheesecake is done when the edges are set but the center still has a slight wobble.
7.  Turn the oven off, crack the door open, and let the cheesecake cool in the oven for 1 hour. This helps prevent cracking.
8.  Remove from the oven, let cool to room temperature, then chill in the refrigerator for at least 4 hours before serving.
___

>[!Note]
>The original recipe card was very fragmented. The ambiguous "1/4" for cream cheese has been interpreted as **1.5 pounds (three 8-oz packages)** to balance the 2 cups of sugar. The baking time of "7 min" was a clear error; a standard time of **45-50 minutes** has been provided.
