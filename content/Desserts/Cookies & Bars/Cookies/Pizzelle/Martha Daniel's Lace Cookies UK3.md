*A classic recipe from Martha Daniel for thin, crispy, and buttery oatmeal lace cookies. This simple batter is chilled overnight and baked on foil, creating a delicate, see-through texture.*
### Author
Martha Daniel
___
## Recipe
| Measurements           | Ingredients                                        |
| :--------------------- | :------------------------------------------------- |
| 1/4 Pound (1 stick)    | [[Butter]]                                         |
| 1                      | [[Egg]]                                            |
| 2 Teaspoons            | [[Vanilla]]                                        |
| 1 Cup                  | [[Old Fashioned Oats]]                             |
| 1 Cup                  | [[Sugar]]                                          |
| 1 Teaspoon             | [[Salt]]                                           |
| 3 Tablespoons          | Sifted [[Flour]]                                   |
| 1/4 Teaspoon           | [[Baking Powder]]                                  |
___
## Instructions
1.  **Prepare the Dough:** In a small saucepan, melt the butter and let it cool slightly.
2.  In a large bowl, beat the egg well. Stir in the vanilla and the cooled, melted butter.
3.  In a separate bowl, mix together all the dry ingredients: oats, sugar, salt, flour, and baking powder.
4.  Add the dry ingredients to the butter and egg mixture and mix well.
5.  Cover the bowl and **refrigerate overnight**.
6.  **Bake the Cookies:** The next day, preheat your oven to **325°F**.
7.  Cover a cookie sheet with a thin layer of foil. **Do not grease it.**
8.  Drop the cold batter by **half-teaspoonfuls**, leaving plenty of room (at least 3-4 inches) for the cookies to spread.
9.  Bake for **10-15 minutes**. Watch them carefully, as they are thin and can burn easily. They are done when they are a uniform golden brown.
10. **Cool:** Let the cookies cool completely on the foil.
11. Once the cookies are cool and crisp, gently peel the foil away from the back of the cookies.
___

>[!Note]
>As the recipe card notes, the batter works best when it's cold, so be sure to put it back in the refrigerator between baking batches.
