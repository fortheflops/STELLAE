# Danish Butter Cookies

> 📜 **Collection:** Gail Collection | ✍️ **Attribution:** Unknown | 📂 **Category:** Desserts
> *A classic, large-batch recipe for Danish Butter Cookies featuring a unique method of folding stiffly beaten egg whites into a rich butter dough. These cookies offer a wonderfully light and crisp texture perfect for any occasion.*

---

| Prep Time | Cook Time | Inactive / Chill Time | Yield / Servings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 25 mins | 8 mins | None | Approx. 7 dozen cookies |

---

### 🔪 Key Equipment
* Electric mixer
* Large mixing bowl
* Clean mixing bowl
* Cookie sheet

---
## Ingredients

| Measurements | Ingredients | Prep / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 pound (4 sticks) | [[Margarine]] or butter | softened |
| 1 cup | [[Sugar]] | granulated |
| 2 | [[Eggs]] | separated |
| 5 cups | [[Flour]] | sifted all-purpose |
| 2 teaspoons | [[Vanilla Extract]] | none |
| Optional | Glazed [[Fruit]] | for decoration |

---

## Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Base
1. Preheat your oven to 360°F.
2. In a large bowl, cream the **1 pound margarine** and **1 cup sugar** together with an electric mixer until well combined.
3. Add the **2 egg yolks** and **2 teaspoons vanilla extract**, and beat until incorporated.

### Step 2: Prepare the Egg Whites
4. In a separate, clean bowl, beat the **2 egg whites** with clean beaters until stiff peaks form. Set aside.

### Step 3: Combine and Bake
5. On low speed, gradually add the **5 cups sifted flour** to the creamed mixture; the dough will be very stiff.
6. Gently fold the **stiffly beaten egg whites** into the dough until just combined.
7. Take a half teaspoon of dough, roll it into a small ball, and place it on an ungreased cookie sheet. Flatten the ball with your fingers or the bottom of a glass.
8. If desired, press a small piece of **glazed fruit** into the center of each cookie.
9. Bake for about **8 minutes**, or until the edges are lightly golden and fragrant.

---

> 💡 **Make-Ahead & Storage:** Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to one week, or freeze the unbaked dough balls for up to 3 months.
