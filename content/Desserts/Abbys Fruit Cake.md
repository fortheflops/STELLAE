# Abby's Fruit Cake

> 📜 **Collection:** UK2 | ✍️ **Attribution:** Abby | 📂 **Category:** Desserts
> *A magnificent, large-batch fruit cake recipe from Abby yielding 11 pounds of rich, moist, and spicy cake. The fruits are cooked in apricot nectar and soaked overnight for incredible flavor.*

## Instructions

### Step 1: Soaking the Fruit
1. Rinse and drain **1 ½ cups golden raisins** and **1 ½ cups dark raisins**. If they seem hard, steam them briefly in a sieve over boiling water to soften.
2. In a large kettle or stockpot, combine the **1 ½ cups golden raisins**, **1 ½ cups dark raisins**, **2 cups dates**, **6 cups candied fruit (mix)**, and **1 ½ cups apricot whole fruit nectar**.
3. Cook over low heat for about **5 minutes**, stirring constantly until heated through.
4. Cover the pot and let the fruit mixture stand at room temperature overnight.

### Step 2: Mixing and Baking
5. Preheat your oven to **300°F**. Line one **9-inch tube pan** and **two 8.5x4.5 inch loaf pans** with two layers of greased brown paper, followed by one layer of greased wax paper. Place a shallow pan of water on the bottom rack of the oven.
6. In a very large bowl, cream the **2 cups (1 lb) butter** and **2 cups sugar** together until light and fluffy. Add the **10 eggs** one at a time, mixing well after each addition.
7. In a separate bowl, sift together the **6 cups flour**, **2 tsp. salt**, **½ tsp. baking soda**, **2 tsp. cinnamon**, **1 tsp. allspice**, **½ tsp. cloves**, and **1 tsp. nutmeg**.
8. Add the sifted dry ingredients to the creamed mixture and stir to combine. Stir in the **2 tbsp vanilla** and **2 cups nuts**. The batter will be very thick.
9. Pour the prepared batter over the soaked fruit mixture in the kettle. Mix thoroughly with a sturdy spoon until everything is well combined and uniform.
10. Turn the batter into the prepared pans. Bake for **3 to 3 ½ hours**, or until a long wooden skewer inserted into the center comes out clean and free of wet batter.

---

> 💡 **Make-Ahead & Storage:** Store the baked fruit cakes wrapped tightly in parchment and foil in a cool place, occasionally brushing with spirits to mature for up to several weeks.
