# Broccoli Crab Dish

> 📜 **Collection:** Dwayne Collection | ✍️ **Attribution:** Unattributed | 📂 **Category:** Entrees
> *A rich and comforting seafood casserole layering tender broccoli, flaked crab meat, and a creamy celery soup mixture, topped with savory stuffing and melted cheddar cheese.*

---

| Prep Time | Cook Time | Inactive / Chill Time | Yield / Servings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 20 mins | 1 hour | None | 8 servings |

---

### 🔪 Key Equipment
* Oblong baking dish
* Mixing bowl
* Measuring cups

---
## Ingredients

| Measurements | Ingredients | Prep / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2 packages | [[Frozen Broccoli]] | cooked |
| 2 cans | [[Crab Meat]] | flaked |
| 2 cans | [[Cream of Celery Soup]] | divided |
| 1 carton | [[Sour Cream]] | divided |
| 1 stick | [[Butter]] | melted |
| 1 cup | [[Water]] | boiling |
| 1 package (8 oz) | [[Pepperidge Farm Stuffing Mix]] |  |
| To taste | [[Cheddar Cheese]] | grated, for topping |

---

## Instructions

### Assembly and Baking
1. Cook the **2 packages frozen broccoli** according to package directions, then layer them evenly in the bottom of an oblong baking dish.
2. Flake the **2 cans crab meat** and scatter them evenly over the layer of broccoli.
3. In a bowl, mix **1 can cream of celery soup** with **half carton sour cream** until smooth. Pour this mixture evenly over the crab and broccoli layers.
4. Melt **1 stick butter** in **1 cup boiling water**. Add this to **1 package (8 oz) Pepperidge Farm stuffing mix** and toss to combine.
5. Mix the second **1 can cream of celery soup** with the **remaining half carton sour cream**. Pour this over the stuffing mixture.
6. Top the casserole generously with **grated cheddar cheese**.
7. Bake at **350°F** for **1 hour** until the cheese is melted, bubbly, and golden brown around the edges.

---

> 💡 **Make-Ahead & Storage:** This casserole can be assembled up to 24 hours in advance, covered, and stored in the refrigerator. Bake directly from the fridge, adding 10-15 minutes to the baking time if needed.
