# Easy Honey-Ginger Teriyaki Flank Steak

> 📜 **Collection:** GailCollection | ✍️ **Attribution:** Unknown | 📂 **Category:** Entrees
> *A tender and flavorful broiled flank steak marinated for hours in a simple, homemade teriyaki sauce made with soy, honey, ginger, and garlic.*

---

| Prep Time | Cook Time | Inactive / Chill Time | Yield / Servings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 10 mins | 15 mins | 3 hours | 4-6 servings |

---

### 🔪 Key Equipment
* Bowl or shallow dish
* Broiler

---
## Ingredients

| Measurements | Ingredients | Prep / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| One | [[Flank Steak]] | Large |

### For the Marinade
| Measurements | Ingredients | Prep / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1/4 Cup | [[Soy Sauce]] | None |
| 2 Tablespoons | [[Salad Oil]] | None |
| 2 Tablespoons | [[Honey]] | None |
| 1 Tablespoon | [[Vinegar]] | Wine vinegar |
| 1 Clove | [[Garlic]] | Minced |
| 1 Teaspoon | [[Ginger Root]] | Minced fresh (or 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger) |

---

## Instructions

### Step 1: Marinate the Steak
1. In a bowl or a shallow dish, combine the marinade ingredients: **1/4 cup soy sauce**, **2 tablespoons salad oil**, **2 tablespoons honey**, **1 tablespoon wine vinegar**, **1 clove minced garlic**, and **1 teaspoon minced fresh ginger root** (or **1/2 teaspoon ground ginger**).
2. Place the **one large flank steak** in the marinade, ensuring it is well coated.
3. Marinate in the refrigerator for several hours until deeply infused with flavor.

### Step 2: Broil and Serve
4. When ready to cook, preheat the broiler.
5. Broil the **one large flank steak** for **4 minutes** per side for rare, or until it reaches your desired doneness and is nicely caramelized.
6. Baste the steak with the marinade every so often while broiling.
7. Let the steak rest for a few minutes after cooking, then slice it thinly on the diagonal.
8. Arrange the slices on a plank or platter, surrounded with potatoes and stuffed mushroom caps if desired.

---

> 💡 **Make-Ahead & Storage:** Marinate the flank steak in the refrigerator for up to 6 hours before broiling for optimal flavor penetration.
