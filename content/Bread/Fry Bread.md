# Fry Bread

> 📜 **Collection:** UK2 | ✍️ **Attribution:** Unknown | 📂 **Category:** Bread
> *A classic recipe for deep-fried bread, often known as Navajo or Native American Fry Bread. This simple dough is fried in deep fat until puffy and golden, perfect for serving sweet or savory.*

---

| Prep Time | Cook Time | Inactive / Chill Time | Yield / Servings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 15 mins | 15 mins | None | 12-16 pieces |

### 🔪 Key Equipment
* Large bowl
* Pastry blender
* Rolling pin
* Deep heavy skillet
* Slotted spoon
* Paper towels

## Ingredients

| Measurements | Ingredients | Prep / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 3 Cups | [[Flour]] | All-purpose |
| 1 Tbsp | [[Baking Powder]] | Sifted |
| 1 tsp | [[Salt]] | Standard |
| 1 Tbsp | [[Shortening]] | Solid |
| ~2 Cups | [[Water]] | Lukewarm, approximately 1 pint |
| As needed | [[Fat]] | Vegetable oil or lard for deep frying |

---

## Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Dough
1. In a large bowl, sift together the **3 cups flour**, **1 tbsp baking powder**, and **1 tsp salt**.
2. Using your fingertips or a pastry blender, cut the **1 tbsp shortening** into the **flour mixture** until it resembles coarse crumbs.
3. Gradually add the **~2 cups lukewarm water**, mixing until a soft dough forms that is similar in consistency to biscuit dough.

### Step 2: Shape and Fry
4. Turn the **dough** out onto a lightly floured surface and knead gently for a minute.
5. Roll the **dough** out to about **½-inch thickness** and cut it into **3-inch squares**. Cut a small hole or slit in the center of each square to help it cook evenly.
6. In a deep, heavy skillet, heat about **2 inches of fat or oil** to **375°F**.
7. Carefully fry the **dough squares** one or two at a time for **1-2 minutes per side**, until they are puffy and golden brown.
8. Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels. Serve warm with honey, powdered sugar, or as a base for tacos.

---

> 💡 **Make-Ahead & Storage:** Best served immediately while warm and fresh, but shaped uncooked dough can be kept covered for a short period.
